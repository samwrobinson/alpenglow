Perinatal Mental Health Therapy in Vermont

Perinatal Mental Health

Pregnancy, Postpartum & Loss

Perinatal mental health therapy in Vermont can be anything from dealing with grief around struggling to conceive, to getting through the first year of your new life with a baby. I'm Amber, a Licensed Clinical Mental Health Counselor, and I specialize in Perinatal Mental Health. I see clients in person at The Village Collective in Richmond and online anywhere in Vermont.

Who I work with

Most of the women who reach out to me say some version of the same thing: something about being a new parent feels off, it isn't what they pictured, and they can't name what's wrong. Sometimes it has a name, like postpartum anxiety or depression. Sometimes it's grief after a miscarriage or a long stretch of infertility. Sometimes it's the way a family member showed up after the birth and nobody agreed on the rules.

If any of that sounds familiar, you're in the right place.

What perinatal means

The term comes from Latin words meaning "around" birth. It covers the time from conception through the first full year after delivery. In practice I take it wider than that. I work with women who are family planning, trying to conceive, pregnant, postpartum, or still unpacking a birth that happened a while ago.

Boundaries with family after a baby

A new baby rearranges every relationship around it. Family members who were easy to be around suddenly have opinions about feeding, sleep, visits, and who gets to hold the baby first. A lot of the couples I work with aren't fighting with their families so much as they're fighting with each other about their families.

We slow this down. We figure out what you need, what you can live with, and what's a hard no, then work on how to say it so it lands. This is often where family-of-origin work comes in, because the boundaries that are hardest to hold are usually the ones we never got to have growing up.

Perinatal support I offer

  • Postpartum anxiety and depression

    For new parents who feel anxious, flat, angry, or lost in the early months and want to understand why.

  • Miscarriage, stillbirth, and infertility

    Grief that the people around you may not see, and support through trying again.

  • Boundaries with family after a baby

    Working out what you and your partner want from your extended family and inner-circle, and how to say it.

  • EMDR

    For birth trauma and older wounds that a pregnancy brought back up.
Working Together

How I work

I'm trained in EMDR and I work from a trauma-focused lens. Day to day that looks like narrative therapy, parts work, and a lot of attention to whether you feel settled enough in the room to do the work at all. We live in a fast-paced world and I aim to create a calm environment, whether we meet in person, online, or outside, so you can slow down and work on what matters to you most.

Sessions are in person in Richmond one day a week and by telehealth the rest of the week. In warmer months I also offer outdoor sessions.

Getting started

We start with a free 15-minute virtual meeting so we can both decide whether we're a good fit. If we are, I'll send intake paperwork through a simple portal and we'll schedule a first session. The first few sessions are about getting to know each other, what brought you in, and what you want to be different.
